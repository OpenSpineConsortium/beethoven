**Before:** nothing of ours could say how long this account's jobs waited for a node on the requeue partition against the primary or secondary ones. The answer to "is `reqp` really slower, and by how much" was a feeling, and the two-queue worker policy greg floated (ask on both partitions, run where a node comes first) had no numbers to rest on.

**After:** `hpcworker queue report [--days N] [--limits]` reads the account's own scheduler history once and prints, per partition and QoS, how many jobs started, their median, p90 and longest wait, how many are pending, were preempted, cancelled or timed out, and the median run time, then one footer line: `shortest median wait: <partition> <qos> (<median>)`. `--limits` adds one `sinfo` with each partition's time limit, availability and nodes by state. It is a maintainer's and lab lead's verb at a terminal; students never see it (no contract line, no panel words).

What it runs on the login node: one `sacct -u $USER -S now-Ndays -X -n -P --format=JobID,JobName,Partition,QOS,Submit,Start,End,State,Timelimit,NodeList` under `timeout 60`, and with `--limits` one `sinfo -h -o '%P|%l|%a|%D|%T'` under `timeout 10`. The header repeats the `sacct` line word for word, so the count it reports can be checked by typing that line. Nothing is written anywhere.

**How:** a new adapter call `adapter_history DAYS` (Slurm: the `sacct` above; PBS and LSF: exit 2 and nothing printed, and the verb says the report is for Slurm sites), `adapter_partitions` now carrying node counts by state, and the `_query` twins that print the line each one runs. The arithmetic is POSIX awk in `bin/hpcworker` (`HPCW_QUEUE_AWK`; the login node's awk has no `mktime`): a Slurm time becomes seconds by the days-from-civil formula and only differences are used, so the zone never enters. A Start before its Submit counts as no wait and is reported; a line that is not a record is left out and counted. Durations read `Nd Nh Nm`, `Nh Nm` or `Nm Ns`. `--days` takes 1 to 90 (default 14). `tests/fakegrid.py` gains a fake `sacct` and `sinfo`; `tests/test_queue.py` plants waits of 30 s to 2 d across `reqp/requeue` and `secp/secondary` with a pending, a preempted, a cancelled, a timed-out and a still-running job, and checks every row, the footer, the one exact `sacct` argv and that no `sbatch`, `squeue` or `scancel` ran, that nothing was written under HOME, the refusals, a PBS site, and the days-from-civil formula against python's `calendar` over a sweep of dates. `framework/README.md` documents the verb, with a sample marked as made-up data.
